### Overclaim risks (read before writing the paper text)

1. **Leakage control is not new for SNN-IDS.** Patel et al. (2026) already use leakage-resistant, capture/scenario-aware splits and train-only transforms. Our distinct points are duplicate-aware feature-group splitting on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and the non-spiking/tree baselines, not leakage control as such.
2. **Cross-dataset and FA-constrained metrics are not new for SNN-IDS.** Karthik et al. (2026) report GAR@FAR=1% and cross-dataset macro-F1 on a CIC-IDS2017 subset. Their setup uses random splits and several tables are labelled "simulated", but the paper should not claim to be the first SNN-IDS work with either feature. Patel et al. also calibrate to a 1% FAR in an appendix.
3. **Tree baselines are not wholly absent.** Zhou & Li list RF and gradient tree boosting (copied from earlier work) and Karthik et al. include RF. "Few prior SNN-IDS studies retrain tree ensembles under the same protocol" is defensible; "none compare" is not.
4. **On-chip energy has been measured before.** Alom & Taha (2017) ran an IDS on a physical TrueNorth chip. Our Loihi numbers are estimates from published per-op figures and must be labelled as such. No verified prior work measured SNN-IDS energy on Loihi.
5. **CSE-CIC-IDS2018 SNN results exist.** Wang et al. (2024) report 98.82% accuracy on a 6-class, 5,000-per-class subset with no described split; useful as a contrast to our leakage-controlled macro-F1.

## (b) Draft paragraph

```latex
\textbf{SNN-based intrusion detection.} Zhou and Li~\cite{zhou2021singlespike} trained non-leaky, single-spike temporal-coded networks on NSL-KDD and AWID and reported higher accuracy than a range of deep and classical learners, although their tree-ensemble figures were taken from earlier work rather than rerun. Most later studies train leaky integrate-and-fire networks with surrogate gradients. Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024convsnn} combined convolutional and spiking layers on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and CIC-DDoS2019, matching an optimised CNN with far fewer parameters, and compared only against neural models. \v{Z}ivadinovi\'c and Simi\'c~\cite{zivadinovic2024resource} trained a small spiking classifier on BoT-IoT samples, and Fesu et al.~\cite{fesu2025comparative} set an SNN against a CNN on NSL-KDD. Karthik et al.~\cite{karthik2026hybrid} reported detection at a 1\% false-alarm rate and cross-dataset scores for a transformer--spiking hybrid, but on random percentage splits. Closest to our concerns, Patel et al.~\cite{patel2026spiketiming} benchmarked 27 neuron and encoding configurations under capture- and scenario-aware splits and found latency coding to matter more than the neuron model; their comparison stays within spiking models. Hardware evidence is thin. Alom and Taha~\cite{alom2017truenorth} ran a detector on a physical TrueNorth chip, and Mehrabi and van Schaik~\cite{mehrabi2024industrial} built a packet-level SNN on an FPGA. What we add is a combination. We control duplicate leakage on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and judge every model, tree ensembles and a spike-input control included, at fixed false-alarm budgets. The frozen detectors are then tested on CIC-IDS2017.
```

(About 235 words. Uses `\v{Z}`/`\'c` so it compiles without inputenc tricks. The transformer--spiking en dash is a compound hyphen, not an em dash.)
